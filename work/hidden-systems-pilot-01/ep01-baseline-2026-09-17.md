## Unresolved: the two sources disagree by ~2×

At 157s AVD, 18 views implies 0.785 h of watch time. The CSV reports **0.3967 h** — a factor of
1.98. Run backwards, the CSV's watch time corresponds to about **9.1 views**, not 18.

Not resolved. The panel is video-level and purpose-built, so it is the one to trust. Worth
checking before this is used for anything: the date-range selector on both views, and whether
the CSV was exported from the channel-level Content tab rather than the video's own analytics.

## What this says

**Retention is not the problem.** 55.6% on a 4:42 explainer is a good number. People who start
the video mostly stay with it.

**Treat 55.6% as a ceiling, not a true read.** About half the views came from outside YouTube's
surfaces — shares and direct links, given 0 subscribers. A referred audience watches much further
than browse traffic. The number will fall when real distribution arrives.

**The bottleneck is distribution.** 310 impressions in 4 days is the whole real-world test.
The constraint is not that viewers leave; it is that almost nobody is offered the video.

**The CTR still says nothing.** 9 clicks on 310 impressions spans "bad" to "fine". No packaging
decision can be made on it.

## Implication for Episode 02

The M02 dynamism pass (77% static, four dead runs) was a real defect measured on its own terms
and was worth fixing. But **this data gives no evidence that retention was Ep 01's limiting
factor**, so the dynamism work should not be expected to move views.

The leverage is in getting shown at all: packaging and topic selection. Episode 02's subject was
chosen on proven demand (Jared Owen escalator 19.7M; Zack D. Films Shorts at 49.3M and 25.9M on
the two specific questions, with no long-form treatment of either). That remains the right lever.

requirement: *"Your video should also be at least 60 seconds long and have at least 100 views."*
78 views short. Any source of views counts toward the threshold.

### This revises the 55.6% figure downward

55.6% is a blend dominated by insider views (the 99.97% channel-page view, launch-day friends).
**Strangers arriving from search watched 37.3%.** That is the number to treat as real, with the
caveat that it rests on 6 views and is not yet interpretable.

The earlier note that 55.6% should be read as a ceiling was correct; this quantifies it.

### Consequence for packaging

If search is the only distribution, **title and topic search-demand matter more than the
thumbnail right now** — search results are scanned with stated intent, not browsed.
The current title, *"Fire Sprinklers Don't Actually Work The Way You Think"*, is a curiosity-gap
title. It does not contain the phrasing people type ("how do fire sprinklers work", "do all fire
sprinklers go off at once"). Worth testing against the Episode 02 title before it locks.
