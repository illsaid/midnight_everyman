# Pilot 01 — title decision

**Working title (locked, pending thumbnail):**

> ## Fire Sprinklers Don't Actually Work The Way You Think

53 characters · vidIQ CTR score **96 / 100**

---

## Why it's the right pick

It is the only candidate that stacks **two independently validated patterns** from the
competitor analysis:

| Component | Source | Index |
|---|---|---|
| "Actually" | Deconstructed, n=7 | **2.46×** |
| "…Than / The Way You Think" | Deconstructed, n=2 | **1.67×** |

Both come from the strongest comparable channel, and it avoids all three measured losers:
"How a &lt;object&gt; works" (0.46×), "The Genius Design of" (0.39×), and the exact 120k-view
title Deconstructed already used for this subject.

**It also satisfies "one promise, three times"** (`packaging.md`) without any rewriting:

| Rung | Content |
|---|---|
| Title | "…don't work the way you think" |
| Thumbnail | *(pending)* — must show the wrong belief, not the mechanism |
| First 8 s | The movie deluge — the belief, shown, then killed at 0:17.8 |

The cold open already *is* the payoff for this title. No script change needed.

**Uncontested.** A YouTube search for this framing returns nothing on fire sprinklers — the
sprinkler results are all lawn-irrigation repair. Nobody owns this angle.

---

## The one real problem: mobile truncation

At 53 characters it survives desktop but not the mobile home feed.

| Surface | Cut | Renders as |
|---|---|---|
| Desktop sidebar / watch | 60–70 | full title |
| Mobile search | ~48 | "Fire Sprinklers Don't Actually Work The Way You…" — fine |
| **Mobile home feed** | **~40** | **"Fire Sprinklers Don't Actually Work The…"** |

That last one is the problem. It reads as *"fire sprinklers don't work"* — a fire-safety
claim the video explicitly refutes. Consequences:

1. **Wrong promise.** The video's argument is that sprinklers work *better and more simply*
   than people assume. The truncation says the opposite.
2. **Wrong audience.** It recruits people expecting an exposé. They bounce, and per the
   convergence rule they degrade targeting for episode two.
3. **Comment-section correction risk** on a life-safety topic.

Mobile browse is where most impressions happen, so this is not a rounding error.

### Two ways to resolve it

**Option A — ship as-is, accept the truncation.** Defensible: the ellipsis signals more text,
and "don't actually work…" is genuinely arresting. You get the highest-scoring title and the
strongest pattern stack. The risk is real but bounded, and title changes are free after
publishing.

**Option B — shorten to 44 characters.**

> **Fire Sprinklers Don't Work The Way You Think**

Truncates to "Fire Sprinklers Don't Work The Way You T…" — clumsy but not misleading, and
survives mobile search intact. **Cost: it drops "Actually", which is the 2.46× word.** That is
a meaningful trade for a formatting fix.

### Recommendation

**Ship Option A.** The pattern evidence for "Actually" is the strongest single signal in the
whole competitor dataset (n=7, 2.46×), and giving it up to fix a truncation is paying too much.
Watch the first 48 hours of comments — if the misread shows up, swap to Option B. Titles are
the one packaging element that is free to change after publishing.

## What this now blocks

The thumbnail. Rung two has to carry **the wrong belief**, not the mechanism — the movie flood,
not the glass bulb. A thumbnail showing a cutaway sprinkler head under this title breaks the
promise chain, because the title says *"you're wrong"* and the picture would say *"here's a
diagram."*

Three concepts needed (A/B/C per `thumbnail-system-v1.md`), all built on the false belief.

## Considered and rejected: "…Don't Actually Work This Way"

> Fire Sprinklers Don't Actually Work This Way — 44 chars · vidIQ **88 / 100**

The idea is sound: make the title point *at* the thumbnail, so the eye has to loop between
them. That deictic move is real and it works. It is just in the wrong slot.

**Why it loses to the longer version:**

| | "The Way You Think" | "This Way" |
|---|---|---|
| vidIQ score | **96** | 88 |
| Validated patterns | 2 — "Actually" (2.46×) + "…You Think" (1.67×) | 1 — "Actually" only |
| Who is accused | **the viewer** | the picture |
| Survives without the thumbnail | yes | **no** |
| Mobile 40-char cut | "…Work The…" (misleading) | "…Work This…" (garbled) |

The deciding argument is the third row. **"The way you think" indicts the viewer's belief;
"this way" indicts a third object.** The contradiction escalator works because it tells the
viewer *they* are wrong — that is the itch. Pointing at an image is a colder statement and
carries no personal stake.

Second: the title has to stand alone on several surfaces — TV, notifications, shared links,
search rows with small thumbnails, embedded suggestions. "This way" strands on all of them.
And it makes the title's meaning dependent on the thumbnail being legible at 320 px, which
is a bet on execution rather than on copy.

### But the instinct belongs in the thumbnail

Deixis works when the text and the image share a frame — which is exactly what thumbnail text
is. So the pointing move survives, one rung down:

| Rung | Carries |
|---|---|
| Title | **Fire Sprinklers Don't Actually Work The Way You Think** — accuses the viewer |
| Thumbnail image | The movie flood: whole ceiling erupting, papers flying |
| Thumbnail text | **NOT THIS** *(or "NEVER HAPPENS")* — points at the image |
| First 8 s | The flood, played straight, then killed at 0:17.8 |

That gets both moves: the title makes it personal, the thumbnail makes it specific, and the
promise is stated three times without repeating itself.
