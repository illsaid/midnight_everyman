# Episode 02 — Generated-media plan

## Purpose

Use four brief cel-like scenes as human-scale resets between dense mechanism
passages. They establish vulnerability, reaction and atmosphere. They do not
explain safety hardware; mechanically exact actions remain deterministic
Remotion graphics.

Each unit consists of one approved first-frame anchor and one motion result.
The anchor and its animation count together as one generated-media unit.

## Rhythm

| Unit | Cues | Approx. screen time | Narrative job |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G-01 incident reversal | 03–05, reprised at 54–56 | 8 s + reuse | Deliver the click promise, then return as documentary evidence |
| G-02 lace approach | 21–22 | 8 s | Put an ordinary shoe in danger before explaining the comb plate |
| G-03 step sag | 36–38 | 10 s | Turn an abstract damaged step into a human balance problem |
| G-04 ordinary ride | 67–69 | 10 s | Restore calm human scale before the final hidden-system reveal |

This produces a cel/diagram rhythm at approximately 0:08, 1:17, 2:29, 3:49
(G-01 reprise) and 4:36. The longest uninterrupted technical passage remains
under roughly eighty seconds and contains multiple camera/state changes.

## Shared visual contract

- Midnight Everyman flat editorial cel style, restrained 1950s/60s print feel.
- Neutral-dominant paper, ink, reference, ink-mute and cutaway palette; no
  photorealism. Avoid large areas of the exact teal, mustard and coral house
  accents so Remotion can own those colours when they communicate mechanism
  state. Incidental muted clothing colour is acceptable when it cannot compete
  with the state overlay.
- 16:9 composition with no embedded text, logos or signage.
- Preserve generous negative space for Remotion labels and evidence overlays.
- Use the approved Observer reference, including his established costume, where
  a principal commuter is visible. Costume colour is identity rather than
  mechanism state; do not create an episode-specific Observer palette unless an
  actual composite demonstrates a readability failure.
- Camera movement must be restrained and readable; no dramatic lens effects,
  whip pans or modern cinematic grading.
- Generate no exposed internal machinery. The model supplies people, cloth and
  atmosphere; Remotion supplies mechanisms and causal proof.

## Unit briefs

### G-01 — Incident reversal

**Source span:** cues 03–05, approximately 7.7 seconds. Reuse an alternate crop
or freeze across cues 54–56; do not generate a second Hong Kong incident.

**Required action:** A crowded mall escalator carries people upward normally,
stops distinctly, then reverses and begins moving downhill faster. Show alarm
and lost balance without injury detail or a pile-up. Keep the step direction and
camera axis unambiguous.

**First frame:** Wide three-quarter view from the lower landing, crowded upward
escalator centred, ordinary retail architecture simplified, one Everyman-like
figure readable near the front third.

**Fallback after two failed motion attempts:** Hold the approved anchor and use
a deterministic direction arrow, freeze, reverse step-band overlay and reaction
cels. The opening must not become an unbounded generation loop.

### G-02 — Lace approach

**Source span:** cues 21–22, approximately 8.25 seconds.

**Required action:** Low three-quarter view of an ordinary commuter's shoes on a
moving step. One loose shoelace rests on the tread and approaches the landing.
The commuter remains unaware. End before the lace reaches or enters the comb.

**First frame:** Shoe and loose lace dominate the lower-right action region;
yellow comb plate is visible ahead but mechanically quiet. Leave the upper-left
clear for the failure label.

**Fallback:** Slow push on the anchor, animate only the step travel and lace
shadow, then cut to the exact comb-plate diagram before contact.

### G-03 — Step sag

**Source span:** cues 36–38, approximately 10.6 seconds.

**Required action:** Medium-wide view along an escalator. One step immediately
ahead settles slightly below its neighbours. The rider notices and recoils or
grips the handrail. Do not show a hole, impact or exposed internal machinery.

**First frame:** Rider in the left third, descending steps leading into depth,
the soon-to-sag step clearly visible in front. Reserve the upper-right for the
failure label.

**Fallback:** Use the approved `fearBrace` cel over the shared mechanism SVG as
the step drops deterministically.

### G-04 — Ordinary ride

**Source span:** cues 67–69, approximately 10.4 seconds.

**Required action:** An ordinary commuter steps onto and rides a calm escalator
without looking down. Coat and handrail move subtly; the ride remains uneventful.
End in a composition that can hold while the hidden devices are revealed.

**First frame:** Side three-quarter view of the entrance, commuter approaching
from stage left, clear sightline to the comb and brush, uncluttered wall behind.

**Fallback:** Stage the approved `inspectForward` and `reliefSigh` cels over the
deterministic step loop.

## Approval rule

Approve all four anchors as a single style/framing contact sheet before any
motion generation. Review the sheet both for internal consistency and for
compatibility with the scene-state table: each plate must leave enough neutral
space and contrast for its anticipated Remotion overlay. Generate one unit at a
time. A motion result is accepted for the human action and continuity only; it
is never treated as evidence for the mechanism.
