[short pause] Failure three is the floor itself.

A damaged wheel or axle can let a step ride too low. The dangerous part is not merely the sag beneath your feet.

It is that the damaged step is still travelling toward the comb, where it can strike the landing — or leave an opening where the floor should be.

Step-sag devices are positioned to detect that drop and stop the machine. Other systems can detect a missing step before the empty space returns to the passenger side.

[thoughtful] Those first three failures begin at one pair of shoes.

[short pause] Failure four involves everybody on the same moving staircase.

A loaded descending escalator is not simply being carried by its motor. Gravity is helping too, and the drive is controlling the descent. The normal brake holds the machine when it stops.

But break the connection between the drive and the steps... and that brake may be holding the motor while the passengers are standing on something no longer attached to it.

That is why long or heavily loaded machines can carry a second brake farther down the drive system — plus a device specifically watching the main drive chain.
