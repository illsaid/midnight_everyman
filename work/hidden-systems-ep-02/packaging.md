## Why this subject

Chosen by proven demand, not by absence of coverage:

| Signal | Figure |

| Long-form treatment of the comb plate or the brushes | **none found** |

The two highest-demand questions about escalators are answered nowhere in more
than 31 seconds. That is the unclaimed angle: proven subject, unserved depth.

It also resolves the danger-frame tension. On this subject the danger is native
rather than bolted on — the grooves, the comb teeth, the side brushes and the
yellow demarcation all exist because escalators trap feet, laces and shoes. We
do not have to reach for peril; the mechanism is the peril.

## Thumbnail moment

**The comb plate, with a shoelace entering it.**

House treatment: flat line art on paper `#EEE5CF`, ink `#242622`. One saturated
element — the comb teeth in mustard `#D5A84C`. Yellow comb plates are a real
safety marking, so the colour is carrying state, not decoration, and house rule 1
holds.

Composition follows the Pilot 01 measurements that landed: subject ink around
16%, negative space ~0.84, the subject about 23% of frame width, nothing in the
duration-badge corner, legible at 160px and in grayscale.

Second A/B arm: the same plate in macro with no lace — teeth meshing into the
step grooves, pure geometry. Tests whether the threat or the mechanism pulls
harder, which is the same question the titles are testing.

## Opening visual — first eight seconds

Top landing. Slow push in on the comb plate. Steps arrive and disappear into it
one at a time, teeth meshing into grooves, and the step surface flattens as it
goes. No cut. One dominant action.

The shot is the thumbnail moving, which is the correct relationship between them,
and it states the episode's subject before a word is spoken.

Narration holds until the third step disappears.
