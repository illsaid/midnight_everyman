# The YouTube script framework, applied — what was taken and what was not

Owner supplied a scripting framework (Hook / Bridge / Body / Embed / Outro,
Promise-Proof-Plan, 20–40 point density, Outcome Over Mechanism) on 14 Sep 2026.

That framework is written for the infoproduct and business-education niche, where
the video is the top of a funnel and the viewer arrived with a problem to solve.
Four of its items transfer cleanly to an animated mechanism explainer. Four would
damage this episode, and the evidence against them is our own, gathered the same
day — not a matter of taste.

`script-v2-vo-v3.txt` implements everything in the first table.

## Taken

| Item | What changed |
|---|---|
| **Hook must reaffirm the click, no branded intro** | Already compliant. v1 opened cold on the failure. |
| **Plan — tell them the roadmap so they commit to the runtime** | **The real gain.** v1 never told the viewer what was coming. v2 states it at 0:25: "four ways this machine can hurt you, and the four separate things built to stop each one." |

## Not taken, with the reason

**"The Embed (CTA), 80–90% — position a tool, lead magnet or service."**
There is no product, no list and no service. Inserting one would mean inventing
something to sell. Skipped entirely. This is the item that most clearly marks the
framework as belonging to a different business model.

**"Outcome over mechanism — viewers click for outcomes, not mechanisms."**
This is the most wrong item for this channel, and our own measurement says so.
The demand we are chasing *is* mechanism demand: "Why Escalator Steps Have
Grooves" — 49,274,248 views — is a pure mechanism question with no outcome
attached. Jared Owen's ordinary-object catalogue is literally "How does a
[OBJECT] work?" at 2.5M–25M. There is no fat loss, no channel growth, no outcome.
The payoff *is* understanding. See `research/competitive-landscape-2026-09.md`.

**"20 to 40 discrete points; rapid-fire insights outperform narrative."**
True for tactics listicles. Fatal here. A mechanism explainer is a causal chain —
the comb plate only makes sense after the gap, which only makes sense after the
step chain. It cannot be delivered as thirty interchangeable points. The same
measurement applies: Jared Owen's 19.7M escalator video is one slow causal
explanation, not a list.

**"Proof — deploy an authority statement, credential, or financial metric."**
We are a faceless channel with four views and no credentials in escalator
engineering. An authority statement would be either false or pathetic. Our proof
is a different and better kind, and v2 uses it in the hook: a dated, named,
officially investigated incident, and a detail specific enough that no one could
invent it — one of two springs clamped by a nut. Specificity is the credential.

## Runtime consequence

| Version | Words | At 154 wpm (Pilot 01's measured rate) |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | 804 | 5:13 |
| **v2, with hook Plan and loop labels** | **851** | **5:31** |
| v2 minus failure three (step level) | 716 | 4:38 |

The Plan and the loop labels cost 47 words — about 18 seconds. That is the price
of the framework's one genuinely valuable item, and it is worth paying.

5:31 sits inside the 5–8 minute launch format (D-026). If the owner holds to a
hard 5:00, the cut is failure three: 135 words, 52 seconds, and the only one of
the four failure modes with no demand evidence behind it. Cutting it lands at
4:38 and leaves a three-failure escalation that still works.

**Recommend v2 at 5:31 intact.** The framework's own logic supports this — it says
viewers commit to a stated runtime when the roadmap is specific. v2 states the
roadmap.

## One caution carried forward

The framework rewards density and punishes filler, which is correct. It also
rewards *speed*, which is where the house voice diverges. Pilot 01's measured
effect came substantially from held beats — "[short pause] Until it isn't." A
framework tuned for rapid-fire tactical delivery will, applied literally, strip
exactly the pauses that make this narrator work. Density yes; tempo no.
