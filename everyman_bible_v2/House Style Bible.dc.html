How the system nests. Four strata, each built only from the one below it. Tokens (E, D, I) are raw values: color, type step, duration, easing. Primitives (C, F) are single-purpose components with no opinion about meaning: a cel, an arrow, a label. Compounds (G, H) assemble primitives into an information object: a bar chart, a kitchen. Archetypes (J, K) are timed compositions of compounds with narration slots. An episode is a list of archetypes and nothing else.
The automation constraint is a design constraint. Nothing in this document may require new illustration or hand keyframing at episode time. Every plate here is either a token, a component with a prop table in section M, or a pose request against the cel library in section C. If a treatment cannot be described as component + props + timing, it does not enter the system.
A Creative thesis §A / 13
A 1959 information-design studio, handed a 2026 render farm and told to explain why things go wrong.

The channel answers curiosity questions about systems people depend on and never look at. Fuel, oil, water pressure, GPS, gearboxes, sewers, the grid. Each episode is a consequence chain: a small ordinary decision, then the mechanism that decision touches, then the scale of what happens next.

Two voices carry it. The narrator is composed, precise and never surprised. The Everyman is silent, curious, slightly overconfident, and physically present for every consequence. The comedy is not in either voice alone; it is in the gap between them. The narrator says protections exist. The Everyman finds the edge of that sentence.

The look is limited animation, not because of budget nostalgia but because limitation is the grammar. A held drawing, a hard cut to a new drawing, and a graphic that does the explaining. Mid-century educational film solved this problem with flat shapes, strong silhouettes, asymmetric composition and restrained ink; those solutions still read at phone size, and they are cheap to produce deterministically.

What is not mid-century is the information layer. Charts build, maps fly, numbers count, cutaways explode, money flows. It is as technically sophisticated as any modern explainer, but it obeys period constraints: flat geometry, three to five colors per scene, drawn arrow language, print texture, no glow, no gradients on data, no bevels, no bounce.

The one idea that makes it a style
The cartoon layer and the data layer are the same layer. Nothing cuts to a chart. Things become charts.

01
Repeatable before beautiful
A treatment that appears twice becomes a component. A treatment that cannot be a component is cut.
02
Stillness is allowed
A strong pose held four seconds under good narration beats constant motion. Holds are specified, not accidental.
03
One idea per beat
Three to five seconds, one dominant visual action, one narration sentence. Never two graphics competing.
04
Dry, never manic
The narrator never jokes. The image does. No zoom-punch editing, no reaction spam, no sound-effect comedy.
B Visual direction §B / 13
StagingAsymmetric by default. The Everyman occupies one third; the information occupies the opposing two thirds; the remaining space stays empty paper. Horizon and floor lines are drawn slightly off-level (0.5–1.5°) so flat shapes read as drawn, not placed.
DepthThree planes only: ground (paper), set (flat clipped shapes), subject (cel + graphics). No perspective grid, no vanishing point. Depth comes from overlap, value and a single ink groundline, never from shading or blur.
FinishGrain at 9% multiply, seed stepped on twos so it resettles with the cut; inset vignette 0 0 95px rgba(31,28,20,.38) at 62%. That is the entire reproduction language. No scratches, no dust, no gate weave.

MODULAR CONSTRUCTION RULES
1. The identity kit is fixed. Vertical egg head; two sparse hair strokes; long rounded nose fused to the profile; dot eyes; single-stroke brows; teal jacket with V hem notch; white collar, narrow black tie; coral trousers; small black wedge shoes; ink outline with mild width variation.
2. Anchor at the feet. Every cel is delivered on a common canvas with the anchor at (0.5, 0.94) and a consistent native height, so poses of different extents occupy one stage position. Registration lives in asset metadata, never in the scene.
3. Generate in families. Related poses are requested on one master sheet in one pass. Cross-sheet generation is the primary cause of identity drift; the alarm sheet proved a six-pose sheet holds.
4. Never edit anatomy in code. No limb rotation, no raster warping, no facial feature offsets. Permitted transforms: translate, uniform scale, rotation up to ±8°, horizontal flip, opacity.
5. Flip discipline. Horizontal flip is allowed because the costume is symmetric except the tie knot, which reads either way. Text or handedness in frame forbids flipping.
FACIAL CONSTRUCTION
Faces from the approved sheet: neutral, narrowed, alarmed. The face is built from five marks — brow, eye dot, nose, mouth, ear crescent — and the brow does most of the work. Eyes never change size; mouth never opens wider than half a head unit except in alarmed.
Expressions are cels, not rigs. In full-body staging the expression is part of the pose drawing. Head-only expression cels exist solely for the reaction close-up archetype, delivered as a separate family anchored at the collar line (0.5, 0.98), so <HeadCel> can swap over a held body.
Nine-face core: neutral, curious, narrowed, pleased, dawning, worried, alarmed, deflated, smug.

FOUR WEIGHTS ONLYHairline 3 px, standard 5 px, emphasis 8 px, structure 12 px at 1920. Round caps and joins everywhere. No dashes except reference lines and no line under 3 px, which disappears on mobile.

I Motion grammar §I / 13 · all values at 24 fps
NO SPRING, NO BOUNCEOvershoot is permitted on exactly two things: a cel landing after a replacement (scale 0.94 → 1.00) and an impact tick. Everything else decelerates and stops.
TWOS FOR CHARACTER, ONES FOR DATACharacter and prop transforms sample on twos (12 fps look). Charts, counters and camera run on ones. The mix is the signature: drawn things step, information glides.
ONE THING MOVESIf the camera moves, the graphic holds. If the graphic builds, the camera holds. Two simultaneous motions are permitted only during a transform (I.6).
CUTS, NOT DISSOLVESCels cut. Scenes cut or transform. Cross-dissolve exists only for historical photographs. Nothing in the system fades in over more than 6 f.
THE HOLD IS THE JOKEComedy is dead air. The gap between the mistake and the narrator's next word is 24–40 f, and nothing moves inside it except grain.

He needs one shape a viewer can name. Canonised: the nose-plus-brow wedge — the nose fuses into the brow ridge so the profile is a single continuous forward-leaning wedge, and the back of the skull is noticeably flat. Recognisable in pure black at 60 px.
3 · HANDS ONE SIZE UP
Hands to 0.30 head units, mitten construction: one thumb, one merged finger mass, a single crease line. Fingers are never individually drawn except in point and count. Hands carry more information than the face at playback size.
4 · LINE WEIGHT HIERARCHY
Currently uniform. Canonise three weights: outer silhouette 6 px, internal costume seams 4 px, facial marks 3 px. Weight variation within a single contour is 10–15%, from a dry-brush terminal at each end — never a uniform vector stroke.
5 · POSES PUSHED 20% FURTHER
Existing poses are too polite for held drawings. Every pose gets a clear line of action, a weight shift onto one foot, and one exaggerated extremity. A held drawing must read as an attitude, not a stance.
6 · COSTUME ANCHOR
One memorable non-negotiable detail beyond the palette. Canonised: trousers 4 cm too short, so a band of sock shows above the shoe in every pose. Cheap to draw, impossible to un-see, and it does the “slightly overconfident” characterisation without a facial expression.

DATA & PROJECTION
Requirement
Specification
Source
Public-domain vector geodata as GeoJSON/TopoJSON — Natural Earth for world, country and coastline layers; national statistical or open-government boundary files for sub-national work. Licence and vintage recorded per layer. Never traced from a screenshot; never live map tiles.
Resolution
1:110m for world and continental framings, 1:50m for single-country, national data below that. Using finer data than the framing needs is the main cause of noisy coastlines.
Projection
Chosen per subject, never defaulted: equal-area for anything where size is the argument, conic for a single mid-latitude country, plate-carrée only for regional insets. Mercator is banned for area comparisons. The projection name is printed in the source footer.
Simplification
Topology-preserving simplification, tuned by eye to the target framing: aim for a coastline whose smallest visible feature is roughly 8–12 px. Islands under 4 px are dropped unless named. Simplify after projecting, never before.
Build step
Projection and simplification run once, offline, into a cached path set committed with the episode. Render is then deterministic and fast — no geo library in the render path.
Geographic or schematic — decide before drawing. Use a GeoMap when real location, distance or extent is part of the claim: where the outage spread, how far the water travelled, which countries hold the reserves. Use a schematic plan (as in G2) when the geography is conceptual — a generic city, a supply chain, an anonymous grid. Never blend them: a schematic map with one real coastline in it reads as a factual claim.
HOUSE-STYLE RENDER PASS
Land: flat Olive 500 at 50%, or Paper 300 for a neutral base. No relief, no hillshade, no gradient.
Water: Teal 300. Lakes only if named or relevant.
Coastline: ink 5 px, round joins. This is the only place in the system where a line is drawn from data rather than by hand, so it gets a 0.6 px random per-vertex jitter to sit alongside the drawn language. Jitter seed is fixed per episode — it must not crawl between frames.
Borders: ink 3 px at 60% opacity, dashed 10 8. Internal administrative borders 2 px at 30%, and only when a region is named.
Region fills: semantic colour from E, flat, no choropleth ramps. Above three shaded classes, switch to labelled markers — a nine-step ramp is unreadable at phone size and off-style.
Labels: map-label spec from D — Franklin 600, 26–30 px, 3 px paper halo, no box, no leader unless the marker is under 40 px away. Maximum six labels on screen. Labels appear after a move settles.
Markers: three types only — ink dot (place), teal dot (working), brick dot with rings (event). Ring diameter never encodes magnitude; a number label does.
Routes: ink 8 px dashed 20 18, drawn at 1,400 px/s, open arrowhead at the destination.
Flows: tapered bands as in G10, width by value, semantic fill, ink outline.
Zoom: glide easing, 24–36 f, maximum 3× per move; labels and markers are re-laid out at the destination scale rather than scaled. Stroke widths are recomputed, never scaled — a zoomed 5 px coastline must still be 5 px.
Disputed or uncertain geography: dashed border in Neutral 400 with no fill on either side, plus a Courier footnote naming the dispute. Never assign a disputed area a semantic colour, never silently pick a side, and never leave a claim unlabelled. Same treatment for data with incomplete coverage — unmeasured regions are Neutral 400 with a “no data” key entry, not zero.

A channel that says “about”, “modelled” and “nobody knows” needs to draw those words. Uncertainty is neutral, not negative: it uses Neutral 400, ink, and the dash/hatch/openness axis — never Brick or Ochre, which are reserved for failure and threshold. An estimate is not a warning.

DOES IT NEED TO MOVE?
Motion is earned by one of four things only: a quantity changing, a sequence in time, a spatial relationship being revealed, or a reaction. If the beat has none of them, the frame holds.
Density veto. If the frame already carries four elements, the new information replaces something rather than joining it. Never animate into a full frame.
The 2-second test. If a viewer would understand the graphic from a still, the animation only controls when they understand it — so keep it under 8 f and stop.
Silence budget. At least 20% of every episode is a held frame. Track it; if a cut list has no holds, the pacing is wrong regardless of how good each beat is.

CONTINUITY BUDGETOne transformation per 60–90 seconds, and never two in succession. Back-to-back transforms stop reading as thought and start reading as software. Between them, cut.
WHAT STAYS CONTINUOUSThe hinge holds position, scale and colour exactly. The Observer, if present, does not move during the transform — he is the second hinge, and his stillness is what proves the world did not reset.
NOT WIPESNo dissolves, no morph filters, no shape-tween plugins. A transformation is: hold the hinge, exit the old parts along one axis, draw the new parts in. If it cannot be described that way, it is not in the system.
