# Visual language

## House style

The target is an original midcentury-commercial visual language influenced by economical UPA-era television design, not a copy of any existing character or program.

Characteristics:

- Simplified, expressive silhouettes
- Sparse linework and selective detail
- Flat, limited colors
- Slightly imperfect drawn contours
- Abstract geometric environments
- Paper grain, restrained vignette and broadcast-era texture
- Poses chosen for readable emotional extremes
- Compositions with strong negative space

## Animation grammar

The system uses limited animation deliberately:

- Hold a strong drawing
- Swap to a new complete drawing at a meaningful action or emotional beat
- Add small whole-cel translation, scale or rotation during a hold
- Use a short anticipation before a large replacement
- Let props and backgrounds carry secondary motion
- Use camera moves to change emphasis, not to disguise weak staging
- Favor 12-fps stepped updates inside a 24-fps composition when appropriate

## What should move

- Whole character cel
- Camera or scene container
- Props
- Graphic shapes
- Text and diagrams
- Texture or light effects, sparingly

## What should not move

- Individually rotated limbs pretending to be expressive drawings
- Facial features sliding independently around the head
- Continuous floating motion on every object
- Arbitrary zooms or bounce effects without narrative function
- Raster-body deformation that breaks the drawing

## Shot rhythm

Each 3-5-second beat should contain one dominant visual idea. A typical beat may use:

1. Establishing hold
2. Small anticipation or camera preparation
3. Replacement-pose change
4. Reaction hold
5. Prop, label or transition that motivates the next beat

## Editorial voice

The canonical tone is authoritative information delivered with dry, understated humor. The audience should sense a wink without losing confidence in the explanation.

- Prefer understatement, precise absurdity and calm observations over punchlines.
- Let Everyman provide occasional visual counterpoint to the narrator.
- Aim for a few memorable comic beats across a 5-7-minute episode, not a joke in every paragraph.
- Place humor after the essential fact or action instruction, never inside language that must remain unambiguous for safety.
- Do not mock the person who made the mistake. Aim the joke at machinery, systems, bureaucracy or ordinary human optimism.
- Remove any joke that lengthens the explanation without adding voice, character or rhythm.

Example register: `The pump, having no opinions on the matter, continues.`

## Thumbnail language

Thumbnails use the same shape language and palette but are optimized for feed-size comprehension: one mistake, one consequence cue, aggressive cropping and zero to three words. See [Thumbnail system v1](thumbnail-system-v1.md).

## References

References should specify a property rather than invite wholesale copying:

- Character economy
- Shape language
- Palette relationship
- Background abstraction
- Line quality
- Timing pattern
- Camera or transition pattern

Approved results should become internal references for later work. The house style should increasingly reference its own assets rather than outside material.
