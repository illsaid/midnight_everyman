# Packaging

Distilled 2 Sep 2026 from three practitioner sources: two Paddy Galloway
interviews and one packaging-review livestream (host "April", producer Joelle).
Where they agree, treat it as signal. Where only one asserts it, it is marked.

Title and thumbnail are **one object**. This runs before the subject is queued.

## Gate 3 — packageability

Select the rung-three title, sketch one thumbnail *moment* and define the opening
visual before the subject enters the production-ready queue. If you cannot, it
is not a video. The content would have been fine; that is a different thing.

Record the selected title, thumbnail moment and opening visual literally in the
candidate ledger; do not represent packageability only as a boolean.

This is a **hard gate** — one of three, alongside touched and opaque. A
documented incident is not a gate; it is a `case_strength` field. See
`subject-selection.md`.

Galloway: *"There's many ideas that have sat on the shelf for me for years that
I still haven't figured out how to package and I just haven't made them."*

## The escalator — our formula sits on rung one

| 1 · Label | 2 · Add the stake | 3 · Add the contradiction |
|---|---|---|
| How a lift brake works | The brake that stops a lift when the cable snaps | Six ropes hold a lift. All six can snap and it still won't fall. |
| How the oxygen mask works | What is actually inside the oxygen mask | Your oxygen mask isn't storing oxygen. It's making it, by setting itself on fire. |
| How a fire sprinkler works | The glass bulb that decides when a building floods | Fire sprinklers don't all go off at once. Films have lied to you for forty years. |
| How an airbag works | How an airbag knows when to fire | An airbag is a controlled explosion 30 cm from your face. |

"How a [OBJECT] actually works" is the same shape as "five productivity tips" —
a category with no angle and nothing at risk. Both sources independently name
that shape as the dead zone.

**Twenty title candidates per video.** Never instantiate the template.

## One promise, three times

    Title  →  Thumbnail  →  First 8 seconds

All three resolve to the same promise, and the third is **shown, not said**.
Asked directly whether visual or verbal click-confirmation performs better,
Galloway answered flatly: *"Visually."* Our Remotion pipeline makes this free —
the thumbnail is built from the episode's component library, so opening on it
animated costs nothing.

The roles are distinct but inseparable:

- **Title:** the logical promise or unresolved human question.
- **Thumbnail:** the emotional moment, contradiction or imminent consequence.
- **First eight seconds:** visual proof that the click will be honored.

Do not paste the full title into the thumbnail. Repeating an essential object
noun is acceptable when comprehension requires it; forcing zero shared words is
not an objective.

## Time allocation

Packaging is a gate, not a fixed percentage of production time. Do not adopt a
51% rule or any other unsupported allocation. Complete the twenty-title pass,
three thumbnail concepts and opening visual before detailed production begins,
then record packaging time separately. If the promise is weak, additional
animation hours cannot repair it.

## Volume and iteration

| | |
|---|---|
| Titles per video | 20 |
| Thumbnails per video | **3 minimum**, genuinely different concepts, A/B tested |
| Repackage a flop at | 2 weeks |

Galloway calls three thumbnails a contract term: *"if we can't do this for every
video, we're not signing the deal."* The variants must be different *moments and
framings*, not one image with three captions.

"No man left behind" — YouTube keeps serving impressions to old videos. A flop
is repackageable, not dead. (Unlike short form, where a bad hook is terminal.)

## Idea selection

- **Validate the framing.** Search it. Has someone made it work? Was it a fluke,
  or repeatable on that channel?
- **Outlier = 3–4× a channel's own average.** Not a big absolute number.
- **Look outside the niche.** Formats transplant; topics don't. Adjacency is
  shared demographic + gear-heaviness + skill structure. Our neighbours are
  restoration, teardown, disaster, forensics and autopsy channels — not other
  explainer channels.
- **The ceiling is the size of the thing.** Ask what this could do at its best
  before making it. This is gate 1 restated as an economic argument.
- **Package broader than the content.** The episode can be as specific as it
  likes; the title should not be.

## Current-market audit

Run a bounded audit before the pilot and after each six-episode test block:

- Prefer recent long-form examples and compare each result with its own
  channel baseline; retain older examples only as evergreen evidence.
- Search the exact viewer promise as well as the mechanism name. Exclude repair,
  trade-training, news and unrelated semantic matches from consumer-demand
  conclusions.
- Record whether success repeats across unrelated channels. One large video is
  a clue, not a standard.
- Audit pacing, audio, visual confirmation and emotional framing without copying
  another channel's surface vocabulary.

Do not continuously chase the market between episodes. The audit sets the
minimum current standard; the channel contract determines what we make.

## The 80% rule — and its second half

Every video should share **80% of its audience** with the others. Not "should
they be interested" — *will* they be. We already satisfy this half.

The half we don't: **do it 80% of the time.** One episode in five is a
deliberate swing that may fail.

Galloway: *"I want more 50s and 500s… I don't want to just hit 200,000 every
time."* A system engineered to hit its median can never produce its outlier.

**The line:** vary the *format*, never the audience. That is the difference
between a swing and a pivot, and it is why this does not contradict
`subject-selection.md` — Deconstructed moved from hand tools to historic
machines and kept the same curiosity.

## What we are deliberately not taking

| Their advice | Our position |
|---|---|
| 20+ minute videos | Rejected. Their own host says he discovers new creators through 6–8 min videos on TV, and Galloway's own successful format was "eight, nine minute breakdowns". |
| Faces as scroll-stoppers | Rejected. We give up a documented mechanism and compensate with jeopardy — the instant before the event. |
| Bright, saturated, AI-enhanced thumbnails | Rejected. But note ~75% of viewers browse in dark mode, and a paper ground is a bright rectangle in a dark grid. We get the benefit without the vocabulary. |
| Chapters | Rejected for us. Galloway: chapters suit tutorials and listicles, not narrative — and they invite a skip straight to beat 4. |

## Numbers to discount

Every source is selling something (an accelerator, a cohort, a tool affiliate).
Treat these as anecdote, not evidence:

- "1,000 views/day to 40,000 views/day from a thumbnail change" — no controls.
- "3M instead of 17M views without that one design choice" — pure assertion.
- "300× his average view count" — the format changed entirely, not one variable.
- "75% of users on dark mode" — no source; widely repeated in creator circles.
- "99% of ideas don't make it" — immediately hedged by the speaker.
- Aggregate agency view counts (10bn, 50bn) — not causal evidence.

The *design insights* survive the discount. The multipliers do not.
