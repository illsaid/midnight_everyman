# Format decision

Decided 2 Sep 2026. Revised the same day after a classification error was found
in the evidence, and again after a second external review. Read the correction
before the decision.

## The decision

| | |
|---|---|
| Aspect | **16:9 horizontal, 1920 × 1080** |
| Runtime | **5–8 minutes** |
| Structure | One object, one mechanism, six beats |
| Rate | **24 fps** |
| Cadence | **UNDECIDED** — see below |

Cadence is not a decision this folder is entitled to make yet. Every earlier
version of this table said "1–2 per week". That number came from competitor
cadence, and competitor cadence does not constrain us. **Your build time settles
it.** Until one episode is built and timed, cadence is blank.

### Frame-rate decision

Retain 24 fps from the validated Midnight Everyman Remotion system. The later
30 fps specification was conventional rather than evidence-based and would
retime existing compositions without improving the editorial proposition.
Frame rate may be revisited only if the long-form pilot exposes a concrete
motion or delivery requirement.

## The correction that matters

An earlier version of this file argued: long-form floors beat Shorts floors,
therefore go horizontal. That argument put **two long-form channels in the
Shorts column.** Mido Explained and Secrets Of Simple Things both publish daily
12–15 minute horizontal video. The comparison was invalid.

## What the verified data says

| Channel | Production | Cadence | Median | n | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deconstructed, single-object era | bespoke 3D, 4–8 min | ~monthly | **365,607** | 10 | yes |
| Casual Navigation | drawn 2D schematic, 6–17 min | every 8–10 days | **57,254** | 50 | yes |
| Mido Explained | stock factory footage, 12–15 min | daily | **2,517** | 50 | yes |
| Secrets Of Simple Things | stock/AI assembly, 10–15 min | daily | **865** | 50 | yes |

All four are long-form. Medians span a factor of 420 *within* the format.
**Format is not the variable.** Three things separate the top two from the
bottom two, and they co-vary in all four cases, so this sample cannot isolate
any of them:

- **Purpose-built visuals vs. assembled footage.** The top two draw their own
  diagrams. The bottom two assemble stock.
- **Cadence.** The top two publish monthly and every 8–10 days. The bottom two
  publish daily.
- **Subject discipline.** Secrets Of Simple Things ran three near-identical
  "overnight charging" videos and two near-identical ATM-PIN videos inside five
  weeks — see `../../research/raw/`.

## Why horizontal anyway

Stated as the weaker claim it is:

- The two verified channels with strong floors are both horizontal long-form
  with purpose-built visuals. That is the model we are copying.
- Mid-roll ads require 8+ minutes; vertical video over three minutes is not a
  Short and receives no Shorts-feed distribution, so the middle ground is the
  worst square on the board.
- The RPM differential is commonly quoted as ~$10 for Education long-form
  against ~$0.33 for US Shorts. **No primary source is recorded for either
  figure.** Treat as folklore, not evidence. See `../../research/sources.md`.

**We hold no verified Shorts median.** The horizontal decision is a reasoned bet
on a production model, not the conclusion of a measured comparison.

## The failure mode nearest to us

Not Shorts. It is Secrets Of Simple Things: faceless, horizontal, 10–15 minutes,
everyday objects and "how X works" titles, returning a median of **865**. It is
an adjacent failure mode, not the same format: its videos are longer, daily and
assembled from stock rather than purpose-built diagrams.

That is precisely the bet. If purpose-built animation and subject discipline are
not worth the gap between 865 and 57,254, the channel does not work.

## What this costs

- **The 2-hour-per-video target is dead.** Six minutes of animation is roughly
  eight times forty-five seconds. Realistic solo build is 6–10 hours early,
  falling as the component library fills. Unverified until the pilot.
- Thumbnails are load-bearing — irrelevant in the Shorts feed, decisive here.
- The script is load-bearing. Six minutes cannot be improvised beat to beat.

## Shorts are not abandoned

They are a distribution tap, not the product. Casual Navigation ran 87 Shorts
against 2 long-form in a 30-day window while its long-form floor held. Cut
Shorts *from* finished long-form assets; never author for Shorts.
