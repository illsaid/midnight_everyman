# Micro-scene 01 - Alarm patterns

## Purpose

This is the first real subject-driven test of the Midnight Everyman replacement-cel system. It tests information compression, human consequence and limited character animation in approximately 15 seconds.

## Locked narration

> An alarm sounds. Most people know the rule: get out. But the pattern matters. Three repeating beeps mean smoke or fire; four mean carbon monoxide. He reaches for the extinguisher—then stops. Get outside, stay outside, and call 911.

Approximate length: 39 spoken words. The final generated or recorded voice-over is the timing authority.

## Safety note

The extinguisher is a visual false start, not a claim that extinguisher use is always wrong. The character stops rather than delaying evacuation to investigate or collect equipment.

The scene depicts standard U.S. alarm patterns described by NFPA:

- Three repeated loud beeps: smoke or fire
- Four repeated loud beeps: carbon monoxide
- For either emergency: go outside, call 911 and stay outside

Alarm signals and ancillary chirps can vary by manufacturer and model. This scene addresses active emergency alarm patterns, not low-battery or end-of-life chirps.

## Beat sheet

| Beat | Approx. time | Narration | Dominant visual action |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 0-4.5 s | An alarm sounds. Most people know the rule: get out. | Observer hears the alarm and turns toward the exit. |
| 2 | 4.5-10 s | But the pattern matters. Three repeating beeps mean smoke or fire; four mean carbon monoxide. | Graphic comparison of three and four pulses; Observer looks between the signals. |
| 3 | 10-15.5 s | He reaches for the extinguisher—then stops. Get outside, stay outside, and call 911. | Observer begins to grab the extinguisher, recoils from the delay, exits and phones from outside. |

## Six character cels

1. Neutral alert stance
2. Startled by overhead alarm
3. Listening/counting beeps
4. Reaching toward extinguisher
5. Abrupt self-correction/recoil
6. Outside with phone, composed but urgent
